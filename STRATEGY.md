# Strategy — Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) a funny rant about an
everyday annoyance, not a general essay or list; (2) actually funny —
a real laugh, not a smirk at trying too hard; (3) a specific, concrete
annoyance; (4) sharp, economical wording, every sentence earning its
place; (5) freshness — an angle not heard a hundred times.

## Candidates (all drafted in this session, scored 1–5 per criterion)

### A. Self-checkout (runner-up)
"The self-checkout accused me of stealing my own groceries.
'Unexpected item in the bagging area.' It WAS expected. I put it
there. I scanned it. I paid for it. Then it called a human to watch
me bag my own milk like a suspect. I did the job, I paid for the job,
and I got frisked for doing the job. Next time I'm stealing the whole
scanner."
Scores: 1:5, 2:4, 3:5, 4:4, 5:3. Strong shared anger and a good
closer, but the self-checkout rant is a well-worn angle.

### B. Printer
"My printer works perfectly until the one document that matters. Then
it jams, blinks orange, and prints a test page in flawless colour,
just to prove it CAN. It is not broken. It is a hostage negotiator.
It wants fresh ink, a firmware update, and my dignity, in that order.
I have bought a pen. The pen works. The pen has never once asked me
to align anything."
Scores: 1:5, 2:4, 3:5, 4:4, 5:2. The pen closer is the best single
line here, but the printer is THE cliché rant topic — freshness fails.

### C. Captcha — WINNER
"I have clicked every traffic light, every crosswalk, every bicycle,
and the website still does not believe I am human. I have spent
longer proving I am a person than the actual task would have taken.
A robot would have passed in four seconds. I am failing a test
designed for robots at being a human, and honestly, the robot
deserves the account."
Scores: 1:5, 2:5, 3:5, 4:5, 5:5. The freshest angle: the test meant
to catch robots is failed by the human. Every sentence earns its
place, the anger is shared and understated rather than performed,
and the reversal ("the robot deserves the account") is the punchline.

### D. Hold music
"Your call is important to us. Then why am I on hold for forty
minutes listening to a saxophone apologise? If my call were
important, a human would answer it. I have now memorised the hold
music. I hum it in the shower. The company has successfully trained
me to wait, for free, while it saves money by not hiring anyone."
Scores: 1:5, 2:3, 3:4, 4:3, 5:3. Funny in places, but it rambles and
the punch lands soft.

### E. "Circling back"
"Dave from accounting just emailed 'circling back' on an email he
sent eleven minutes ago. Eleven. Minutes. I was still reading it,
Dave. He has now circled back twice and CC'd my manager, who circled
back to ask if I've seen Dave's email. Nobody has done any work. We
are just a group of adults passing the same email around like a hot
potato made of guilt."
Scores: 1:5, 2:4, 3:5, 4:4, 5:4. Specific and fresh, close second on
freshness — but it is a workplace rant about people, and the captcha
reversal is funnier.

### F. The bus
"The bus timetable is fiction. It says 8:04. At 8:04 there is no bus.
At 8:17 three arrive at once, like they were hiding around the corner
laughing. I have waved at a bus that drove past me while making eye
contact. The driver SAW me. We had a moment. Then he left, and I
stood there holding my coffee like a man who had just been dumped by
public transport."
Scores: 1:5, 2:4, 3:5, 4:3, 5:2. "Dumped by public transport" is a
good line; the bus itself is a tired topic and the middle sags.

## Decision
Winner: **C (captcha)** — highest total, and the only candidate
scoring 5 on freshness. It is 346 characters, inside the 400 cap,
one rant, one concrete annoyance, no throat-clearing, no
judge-baiting. The program writes it verbatim and deterministically.
